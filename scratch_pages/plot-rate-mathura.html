Mathura Plot Rate 2026: The actual amount one has to pay, and the value one receives for it.

For those who have already had a weekend scrolling the property listings in Mathura, then you know the confusing part. One advertisement states that the cost of 100 gaj plot is Rs. 9 lakh. The next number is 35 lakh. They both label themselves "MVDA approved. Both offer 24 hours security, wide roads and parks. So what is the true plot rate of Mathura?

The answer is that there is no single rate and the difference between the lowest rate and the highest rate in Mathura for a plot on sale is not a random one. It's more or less a mirror image of the question: Does a piece of land have the approvals and the development it says it does? This page explains current market prices, why Aakruti Village is not priced at the bottom of the market and how to interpret any listing before you invest a single penny.

Aakruti Village, developed by Earthousing, is a builder speaking about their pricing and market. The other numbers that follow from other projects are public listings and can be verified on portals like 99acres, RealEstateIndia and SquareYards as of mid-2026.

The odds are even and a pattern emerges. The rates for plots in the outer tehsils of the district like Chhata, Govardhan, Akbarpur and Kosi range from about 9,000 to 24,000 per square yard. The sale price of 100 square yard land in these pockets is generally 9 lakh to 24 lakh. The closer you are to Vrindavan and the closer you are to the inside the higher the rate. Projects close to the NH-2 roadway have a starting price of Rs 24,900 per sqy near the NH-2 and increases from there based on plot location and development.

The price of Aakruti Village is Rs. 35000 per sq. yard. That's a step up from much of the market and we don't want to pretend that that's not the case just by putting a "best price" sign up there. A lower price does not necessarily mean the cheapest listing if the land is sold on a power of attorney, or if the roads are not approved or if the road shown in the brochure photo is still a field. The mathura plot rate you pay purchases different things at different rates and that's what is described in the rest of this page.

Just one word about the units, since people tend to get confused on this. A gaj is a square yard. So 100 gaj and 100 sq. yards is the same area which is approximately 900 sq. feet. A seller is quoting a 100 gaj plot in Mathura price, which is calculated on 900 square foot patch. Remember to always compare on a per square yard basis as plot sizes will differ and the headline rate will be misleadingly high or low.

There is a lot of inexpensive land in the district. Some of it is also really risky. The 40,000 rate is a reflection of four aspects of which you won't get 12 lakh on a roadside listing.

The first one is a delayed approval. Aakruti Village has got the approval of Mathura Vrindavan Development Authority (MVDA approval number [INSERT MVDA APPROVAL NO.]). This is significant since many of the cheaper plots in the district have weaker tenures. Some are 143-C conversions only. Some reference an "applied" or "in process" number, this is not a sanctioned layout. Some are part of the agricultural land that is for-sale but has not undergone any conversion. If you buy the plot into a layout which has been approved by the MVDA, it belongs to a sanctioned residential layout, the registry is clean and a bank will be able to give you a loan, because both the plot and the layout are approved.

The second is development which is already in place. The boundaries, parks, open spaces, electrical lines, water supply lines, drainage lines and sewer lines (except for the ones shown in the plan), and internal RCC roads are included in the price. On a "cheap" plot, ‘developed' often means ‘developed later', and sometimes ‘developed half-way' by a society that had run out of money. The years wait or one pays for the infrastructure and the cheap plot was not so cheap.

The third is the paper and the register. No need to chase mutations and registrations after paying, it is done when you buy the agricultural khasra number. Be sure to confirm the actual break down of registry charges and stamp duty in your booking, stamp duty is a state tax and will be paid by the buyer.

The fourth one is location and timing. Land that is closer to Vrindavan or within a developing corridor is more expensive per sq yard now and is generally transacted more rapidly, hence the higher rates of land on the Vrindavan side of the district.

If price is the sole filter, Aakruti Village is not your plot and we'd prefer you to know that here than on site visit. If clean title, completed development and the opportunity for a home loan is important to you the 35,000 rate is working hard.

What does MVDA approved really mean and how do sellers overdo it?

This is the one most crucial phrase in Mathura listings, so it is advisable to slow down on this.

Mathura Vrindavan Development Authority is the planning authority for Mathura and Vrindavan area. Once it approves a development plan including the road widths, open spaces and land use are all documented on file. A plot within a fully sanctioned MVDA scheme does not have any doubt as to its regulatory basis, thus enabling that plot to be treated as normal collateral by banks.

The problem is that "MVDA approved" is used on items which are not a sanctioned layout. Look out for three gentler claims. An applied number is not a 'approved' number. You may be offered a plot in the part that has not been approved in Phase 1 and this is Phase 2. In addition, there is a step known as a land-use conversion that can be done under the UP land revenue rules (143 or 143-C) which is not the same as a sanctioned residential township layout. Not all of these are necessarily frauds. However, they are not the same product as an approved plot, and they shouldn't necessarily be the same price.

Therefore, before you compare any two prices, get the actual number of approvals and the stage that your plot is on by asking the seller, and check the approval. 12 lakh plot with clean MVDA number in completed scheme would indeed be a good deal. A 12 lakh plot with an ‘applied’ number on unconverted land is a transaction with different words.

Located close to the major temples, the Chandrodaya, Prem Mandir and the Banke Bihari and ISKCON corridors, is close to the land, and the supply is limited, making it a premium location.

The average rate of the whole of Mathura is lower because the land available for development is more diffuse in the outer tehsils of Mathura. Which's why the saying "Mathura is cheaper than Vrindavan" is true on average and misleading when taken literally. A well located fully approved plot in the Mathura area, near to the Vrindavan side is more expensive than the plot located far away in Vrindavan, Jait or Chhata. The district label is not quite as descriptive of location, approval, and development stage.

The bottom line for a buyer is this. Shopper in Vrindavan and paying for being walking distance to the famous temples is what your priority is. If you are looking for a clean plot with approval for development and have space to construct, you will probably find better located plots in Mathura at a lower rate compared to central Vrindavan.

A simple checklist is a lot of grief. Before you seriously accept any Mathura plot rate without getting proper answers to these.

Ask . . . what does the registry actually say? You don't want a sale on power of attorney, a stake in undivided agricultural land, you need a registered sale deed in a sanctioned layout.

Request for approval number and check phase. Match to a particular plot, not to a different stage of the same project.

Inquire about what's already constructed, not what's promised. Roads, drainage, electricity and water are either built or a cost you are bearing in the future.

Inquire if a bank would loan on it. If the title isn't getting funded by a mainstream bank, it is saying something about the market.

Check with them for their developer's name and what they have provided. A builder who has finished and occupied projects is a different risk than an anonymous listing that has a mobile telephone number.

Test those two lists with a 12 lakh list and a 35 lakh list and the price difference typically will no longer feel like a discrepancy, and it will feel more like an explanation.
